Figurative Language

🌈 Figurative Language Garden

Learn how writers use creative words and expressions to make ideas more vivid, meaningful, and fun.

SIMILE

A simile compares two different things using "like" or "as."

  • "He runs like a cheetah."
  • "Her skin is as smooth as silk."
  • "The stars shine like diamonds."
METAPHOR

A metaphor directly compares two different things by saying that one thing is another.

  • "Her smile is sunshine."
  • "Time is a thief."
  • "The classroom was a zoo."
PERSONIFICATION

Personification gives human qualities, feelings, or actions to animals, objects, or things that are not human.

  • "The sun smiled down on us."
  • "The wind whispered through the trees."
  • "The flowers danced in the breeze."
HYPERBOLE

Hyperbole is an exaggerated statement used to emphasize an idea or feeling. It is not meant to be taken literally.

  • "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse."
  • "I've told you a million times!"
  • "My bag weighs a ton."
IDIOMS

An idiom is a word or phrase whose meaning cannot be understood simply by interpreting each word literally.

  • Break the ice — to make people feel more comfortable in a social situation.
  • Spill the beans — to reveal a secret.
  • Hit the books — to study seriously.
Types of Nouns

📚 Noun Neighborhood

Discover words that name people, places, things, animals, and ideas.

A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, animal, or idea.
Person — teacher, student, mother Place — school, classroom, Philippines Thing — book, pencil, table Animal — dog, cat, bird Idea or feeling — love, happiness, freedom
COMMON NOUN

Names a general person, place, animal, or thing.

teacher, school, dog, book
PROPER NOUN

Names a specific person, place, organization, or thing.

Maria, Cebu, Philippines, Monday
CONCRETE NOUN

Names something that can be experienced through the five senses.

chair, apple, flower, music
ABSTRACT NOUN

Names an idea, feeling, quality, condition, or concept that cannot be physically touched.

love, honesty, courage, happiness
COLLECTIVE NOUN

Names a group of people, animals, or things as one unit.

team, family, flock, audience, class
COMPOUND NOUN

A noun formed from two or more words combined into one name.

classroom, toothpaste, bus stop, mother-in-law
